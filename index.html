About

I am a Ph.D. graduate (2026) from the School of Intelligent Software and Engineering at Nanjing University, where I was supervised by Prof. Zhenyu Chen. My research sits at the intersection of software testing and large language models: I build methods that make LLM-generated tests more reliable, compare and combine LLMs with search-based software testing (SBST), and design testing techniques for AI-based software such as multi-modal and text-to-image systems.

During my Ph.D., I spent a year (Feb 2025 – Feb 2026) as a visiting researcher at University College London, working with Prof. Earl T. Barr on search-based software testing and LLM-based unit test generation. I completed my Ph.D. in September 2026 and am now looking for postdoctoral research opportunities and open to new collaborations in software engineering, LLM4SE, and software testing.

Research Topics

LLM-based Unit Test Generation & Repair

Co-evolving generation and repair so LLM-written tests compile, pass, and actually catch bugs (TestART), and benchmarking how well LLMs generate class-level tests (TestBench, the ISSTA fine-tuning study).

Search-Based Software Testing × LLMs

Understanding when search-based software testing (SBST) still beats LLMs at unit test generation, and how to combine the two (SUMMIT) rather than treating them as competitors.

Testing AI-based Software

Testing techniques for AI systems themselves — cross-modal testing for text-to-image software (ACTesting), visualization-based testing for deep neural networks (DVTest), and adversarial evaluation of testing ability (MetaA).
